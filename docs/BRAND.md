# Rios Brand

## 1. Brand essence


**Rios is a life-first planner for days that change.**


Rios means in spanish rivers. Life flows like rivers, changes fluidly, your planner should do. 

The brand starts from a simple observation: planning is useful, but life rarely follows the plan exactly. Most planners are good at recording intentions; Rios is designed around what happens next.

Its central idea is:

> **Most planners are built for making a plan. Rios is built for changing one.**

Rios should never feel like another productivity system telling people to optimize themselves. It is a tool that gives structure without turning the structure into an obligation.

The emotional promise is not “get more done.”

It is:

> **Make a useful plan, reshape it when life moves, then get on with your day.**

That leads naturally to the broader brand line:

> **Less time planning. More time living.**

And the philosophical expression:

> **Life flows. Your plan should too.**

---

# 2. The brand tension

Rios lives between two qualities that normally pull apart:

**Structure ↔ flexibility**

It provides enough structure to make the day understandable, while refusing to pretend the day will stay fixed.

That same tension appears throughout the brand:

**Digital precision ↔ human softness**  
The interface is engineered and highly considered, while the brand world can contain paper texture, a frog, landscape references, sunrise and sunset.

**Serious product ↔ light personality**  
The planner itself should feel credible and premium. The communication can occasionally be witty, warm or surprising.

**Sophisticated engine ↔ simple mental model**  
Underneath, Rios can contain recurrence, calendar import, direct manipulation, tracked history, zones, overlaps and complex scheduling logic. The user should experience:

> things → time → flow → what matters → what repeats

The complexity belongs in the system, not in the user's head.

---

# 3. Positioning

The strongest category positioning is:

> **The planner for days that change.**

This is stronger than leading with “spatial planner” because it starts with the user problem rather than the mechanism.

Spatial planning is the **design answer**.

Rios' hierarchy of messages is therefore:

**Problem / positioning**

> The planner for days that change.

**Design thesis**

> Most planners let you edit a schedule. Rios is designed around reshaping one.

**Product mechanism**

> A spatial planner where your day behaves more like a flexible canvas than a static timetable.

**Brand philosophy**

> Life flows. Your plan should too.

**Consumer benefit**

> Less time planning. More time living.

These should not all appear together every time. They serve different purposes.

---

# 4. The design argument

For design awards, press, portfolio and product storytelling, Rios has a stronger argument than simply “a nicer daily planner.”

The thesis is:

> **Rios is an attempt to rethink the daily planner from first principles.**

Planning is inherently visual, spatial, and tactile, but conventional digital calendars still largely represent a day as fixed blocks attached to a clock.

Rios starts from another assumption:

> **Plans change.**

The day therefore becomes something you can manipulate directly.

Tasks can move spatially. Several tasks can move together. Later plans can cascade when something changes. Overlapping plans can occupy parallel lanes without becoming unreadable. Different views expose the same underlying day in whatever representation is most useful.

That produces a useful design phrase:

> **Let your calendar be your canvas.**

Not necessarily the consumer tagline, but a strong expression of the interaction model.

---

# 5. Zones: time with meaning

A major part of the Rios model is that **clock time alone is poor context**.

“14:00–17:00” is abstract.

“Work” immediately communicates something about where the person is, what kinds of tasks belong there, what constraints exist and how that part of the day feels.

Zones turn time into meaningful contexts:

Morning  
Work  
Errands  
Home  
Evening  
Wind-down

Users can define their own.

This helps Rios feel closer to how people experience days in real life. A day is not simply twenty-four equally meaningful hours. It is a sequence of contexts.

So the brand/product thought is:

> **Rios organizes time around life, not just around the clock.**

---

# 6. What Matters

Rios deliberately avoids conventional productivity language such as “P1”, “urgent”, rankings or aggressive prioritization.

A task can instead be marked **Important** with a star.

That means:

> “I want my day to preserve this.”

It does not mean:

> “This must happen at all costs.”

The separate **What Matters** mode then becomes a lens over the entire day.

The preferred icon for that mode is:

> **≋**

The semantics become very clean:

**★** = this particular task matters  
**≋** = show me what matters

*Update 2026-09-28:* in the app, the What Matters toolbar action uses the
star itself (outline when off, filled when on), so the lens and the tasks it
keeps share one symbol. ≋ remains the logo mark.

When activated, What Matters should not feel like applying a database filter. The rest of the day physically releases.

Non-important items soften, lose support, fall downward and disappear. Empty zones collapse. Important tasks remain anchored. The layout settles with more breathing room.

The feeling should be:

> **relief**

rather than:

> “filter applied.”

A useful brand sentence is:

> **What Matters doesn't hide things. It lets the rest of the day fall away.**

---

# 7. Brand personality

The target character is approximately:

> **70% calm editorial utility  
> 20% tactile organic flow  
> 10% playful humanity**

Rios should feel considered, confident and quiet.

Not sterile.

Not childish.

Not productivity-bro.

Not overly wellness-coded.

Not “AI SaaS.”

Not gamified.

The frog, humor, rooster/owl cues and occasional playful moments are useful precisely because the product underneath remains serious.

The rule is:

> **Funny situation. Serious product response.**

For example, in the film the frog can get caught in an overlong laptop task, check the time, experience a chaotic day, and eventually go to bed. But Rios itself should never behave like a joke.

# 8. Visual identity

The identity is built around a small number of primitives rather than a large decorative system.

Dot (droplet) in product represents a captured idea in inbox or task in task view (circle with icon inside)
Line  always soft rounding it's like wave can take many forms (wave)   (in product it actually reprents a pill a task in spatial view) line is like rounded rectangle with icon (so stretched dot) 
Multiple dots is frequency, patter, consistency (github like grid is how tracked items are viewed)


### Dot → line → pattern

The core grammar is:

> **Dot = a thing / potential / unscheduled**  
> **Line = something placed in time / duration / flow**  
> **Grid = repeated observation / history**  
> **Logo = the compressed expression of those same primitives**

This creates unusual product-brand coherence.

An Inbox item can be a dot.

Scheduling it stretches it into a line.

The line moves through the day.

Repeated observations become a grid.

Lines gather into the Rios mark.

This should remain a genuine system rather than being used decoratively everywhere.

---

# 9. Logo

The primary symbol is three restrained parallel flowing lines.

Essentially ≋

They should feel like:

rivers  
currents  
task lanes  
time  
movement

without literally drawing a river.

The lines should be gently curved rather than straight or strongly wavy.

The target is:

> **80% structured / 20% organic**

Too straight and the mark resembles a menu, database or generic data icon.

Too wavy and it becomes decorative or childish.

The lines may have different lengths in motion and can move independently while remaining part of one current.

That leads to the central motion principle:

> **Independent, but connected.**

Each line can lead, lag, stretch, compress and drift while sharing an overall direction.

# 11. Color system

The brighter early palette used lavender, periwinkle/blue and teal.

A more mature direction emerged around the muted trio:

**Flow** — lavender  
**Dusk** — muted blue/slate  
**Moss** — muted green

This better supports the broader brand world: frog, plants, paper, sunrise/sunset, life-first positioning.

The hierarchy is approximately:

**Flow** = signature emotional accent  
**Dusk** = structure and depth  
**Moss** = grounding and natural warmth

Dusk should be kept slightly lighter than some early versions so it does not dominate the other lanes.

The brighter palette does not necessarily disappear. It can become the higher-energy product palette for active states, selected controls or moments where stronger contrast is needed.

So:

> **Muted palette = brand atmosphere**  
> **Brighter palette = product energy**

---

# 12. Sunrise, sunset and the day cycle

Time itself can become a brand device.

Rios has a natural relationship with the cycle of a day, so sunrise and sunset are not arbitrary trendy gradients. They carry meaning.

The preferred treatment is **abstract atmospheric gradients**, not literal landscape imagery.

Morning can emerge from:

deep navy  
indigo  
dusty violet  
peach  
amber  
soft warm light

Evening can reverse into:

warm orange  
dusty rose  
violet  
deep indigo  
navy

No literal sun disc is necessary.

No clouds.

No photographic horizon.

It should feel like:

> **the sensation of a sky changing, expressed as color**

This gives Rios an authored recurring device across the website, film, launch assets and transitions.

It can also reinforce the idea that the interface exists inside a lived day rather than in abstract productivity space.

---

# 13. Paper texture

The brand world can use a faint warm paper texture.

Its purpose is not nostalgia.

It provides:

warmth  
tactility  
human imperfection  
contrast against the precise app UI  
an editorial quality

The texture should be extremely subtle—more atmosphere than visible material.

The rule developed in the conversation is useful:

> **Paper belongs to the world. Vector belongs to the brand elements.**

So:

backgrounds may have subtle grain  
lanes remain crisp vector shapes  
the frog remains vector  
icons remain vector  
logo remains vector

Avoid sprinkling artificial grain inside every object.

That would introduce a second visual language and weaken the clarity of the system.

---

# 14. The frog

The frog is not intended to become a conventional mascot dominating the identity.

It is better thought of as a:

> **quiet recurring companion**

The original frog had a stronger black-outline cartoon style than the rest of the visual world. The preferred direction is to unify it through:

soft charcoal rather than harsh black  
warm off-white body  
simple flat vector shapes  
slightly imperfect contours  
minimal facial expression  
restrained shadows  
no stippled internal texture

It can appear selectively in launch films, empty states, brand illustrations or occasional moments of personality.

The frog should never make the product feel childish.

Its role is to make the brand feel human.

# 15. Illustration world

Supporting illustrations can contain:

plants  
rocks  
river-like lanes  
frog  
simple environmental objects

But the environment should remain sparse.

The lanes are the dominant device.

Plants and rocks are punctuation.

The frog is a character moment.

Avoid turning Rios into a fully illustrated fantasy landscape. That would move the product away from premium software and toward a storytelling/children's aesthetic.

# 16. Icons

The working direction is **Phosphor** for the general UI icon system.

It fits because it is:

soft  
friendly  
cross-platform  
broad enough for a planner  
available in multiple weights  
less rigid than Lucide/Tabler

Use a consistent regular/medium weight rather than very fine strokes.

Signature concepts can use custom icons.

The major custom semantic relationship currently is:

**★ Important**  
**≋ What Matters** (in-app toolbar: ★, see §6)

The flag was rejected because it communicates priority.

Sparkles were rejected because they increasingly signal AI.

# 17. Task state grammar

The task system should remain visually orthogonal rather than inventing completely different styles for every state.

The emerging grammar is:

**Normal**  
Filled geometry using the task/category color.

**Imported**  
Hatched or striped geometry indicating provenance from another calendar/source.

**Unlabeled**  
A new task with no label yet: a reserved pale neutral that no label may use,
so a fresh task never looks already categorised.

**Important**  
The normal task treatment plus a small star sitting on the pill's edge, and a
title gently tinted toward the brand accent.

**Completed**  
Recedes: a pale tint of its own label (never a flat grey that could read as
unlabeled), a faded title, and a strikethrough distinct enough to read.

Important should remain the strongest small semantic signal.

Imported should be informative but quiet.

Completed should visually leave the active day.

---

# 18. Motion language

Motion is a major part of the identity.

The core verbs are:

**stretch → drift → gather → release → settle**

Motion should feel:

fluid  
quiet  
physical  
connected  
slightly springy  
never bouncy or cartoonish

The system should suggest weight, inertia and resistance without becoming skeuomorphic.

Top-level navigation changes space.

Modes transform the current space.

That yields the rule:

> **Navigation changes space. Modes transform space.**

Examples:

Day → Inbox = subtle page movement  
List → Timeline = transformation of the same day  
Edit = controls emerge in place  
What Matters = physical release of existing content

---

# 19. Completion and deletion

Rios should avoid conventional gamified celebration.

No confetti.

No emoji applause.

No achievement explosion.

Completion should feel:

> **resolved → released → settled**

One strong direction is to connect it to the water/dot system.

A task completes, settles, and releases 2–5 subtle bubbles/droplets. Those droplets echo the bubble trail already used when logo waves move.

For tracked behavior, the line could briefly contract into a dot, visually connecting:

> planned duration → completed observation

Deletion should have a related but different physical behavior.

Completion = **bubbles / continuation**

Delete = **puff / dissolution**

A deleted task might compress and create a small neutral puff of 4–7 soft circles before disappearing.

Both effects can be procedural rather than Lottie animations, allowing them to adapt to position, color and context.

# 20. Sound and film personality

The Shipaton film can be slightly funkier than the long-term product marketing.

The current concept uses:

**rooster → energetic day → owl**

The soundtrack should be punchy, repetitive and hook-driven rather than calm ambient product music.

A short motif should become an earworm.

The rooster acts as the opening sonic punctuation.

The owl closes the day.

These are playful devices, but they work because the interface remains polished.

The day arc is:

> dawn → plan → disruption → reshape → focus → act → track → capture → prepare tomorrow → evening → sleep → logo

This full cycle makes the brand feel complete.

# 21. Brand film

The phone/product remains the visual anchor while the surrounding world changes.

A central story is:

The frog plans the day.

A laptop task takes longer than expected.

The schedule becomes crowded.

Conventional calendar representation becomes difficult to read.

Rios reveals the same day spatially.

The user moves tasks directly.

The schedule cascades.

Overlaps remain readable.

Zones clarify contexts.

What Matters releases the noise.

The day continues.

The frog completes things, captures something by voice, arranges tomorrow, then switches off.

The interface enters dark mode as the environment moves into night.

The task lanes eventually detach and reform as the Rios logo.

The viewer realizes:

> **the logo has been the day all along.**

That is one of the strongest pieces of brand/product integration.

# 22. Voice

Rios language should be plain, calm and confident.

It should rarely sound like a productivity app.

Avoid:

“crush your goals”  
“maximize productivity”  
“never miss a task”  
“unlock your potential”  
“stay on top of everything”

Prefer:

> **Plan your day. Live your life.**

> **For days that change.**

> **Focus on what matters.**

> **Arrange it. Rearrange it.**

> **Switch off for today.**

> **No streaks. No pressure.**

> **Less time planning. More time living.**

The strongest writing usually contains one clear idea rather than several marketing claims.

# 23. Product philosophy

Rios should resist productivity conventions that conflict with the brand.

No shame-coded failure states.

No artificial streak pressure.

No “you failed today.”

No unnecessary rankings.

No engagement tricks.

No forced account.

No onboarding obstacle course.

No feature treadmill simply to justify recurring revenue.

The plan is intentionally provisional.

That can be summarized as:

> **The plan is a tool, not a verdict.**

---

# 24. First-run experience

The preferred onboarding is essentially **no onboarding**.

No multi-slide introduction.

No required sign-in.

No configuration questionnaire before value.

Rios opens directly into a functional day.

Disposable tutorial tasks can teach interaction from inside the actual product:

> **Move me around**

> **Long-press anywhere to add something**

> **Star this to focus on what matters**

> **Make the day yours — edit your zones**

The user learns Rios by touching Rios.

The underlying principle:

> **Experience first. Explain when explanation becomes useful.**

Email collection, if used at all, happens later and remains optional.

---

# 25. Commercial identity

Rios is positioned as a **premium product without premium pricing mechanics**.

The intended commercial promise:

> **Pay once. Yours forever.**

No ads.

No artificial free-tier limits.

No recurring subscription for the core planner.

After the trial, the product can become read-only / creation-locked until the user purchases.

The distinction for future services should remain explicit:

> **The core product is yours. Ongoing services are optional.**

Hosted AI, cloud services or other capabilities with genuine recurring infrastructure cost may eventually be separately priced.

This avoids creating an implied promise that every future hosted service is free forever.

The likely trial direction is **21 days**, giving the user enough real-life variability to understand whether Rios fits their days.

---

# 26. Brand architecture

The current primary URL direction is:

> **getrios.app**

rather than embedding “planner” into the domain.

The domain carries the brand.

The positioning explains the category.

That leaves room for Rios to develop without turning the URL itself into a functional descriptor.

Longer term, Rios could potentially sit inside a family of quiet personal software, but the planner itself should not become a super-app.

The broader philosophy could eventually be:

> **Quiet personal software for everyday life.**

Each product would be focused, complete and restrained.

---

# 27. What Rios should feel like

When somebody encounters Rios, the desired impression is not:

> “That's a cute planner.”

Nor:

> “That's a highly optimized productivity system.”

It should be closer to:

> **“Someone reconsidered how a day planner should actually behave.”**

Then, after using it:

> **“It moves the way I expect my day to move.”**

And aesthetically:

> **calm, tactile, fluid, thoughtful, quietly distinctive**

The product should feel sophisticated without displaying sophistication for its own sake.

---

# 28. The core brand system in one sentence

Rios can ultimately be reduced to:

> **A life-first planner for changing days, built around flow rather than rigidity — expressed through dots becoming lines, lines becoming days, and days continuously reshaping around life.**

And the simplest external expression remains:

# **Rios**

### **Less time planning. More time living.**
